## COPY-READY POST

The best engineer in the room shouldn't always be the one leading it.

When I started leading the Web and AI Automation teams at AKIJ iBOS, I jumped into every hard problem myself. Fastest fix, my call, done. The work shipped. The team stopped growing.

So I changed how I show up.

- I stopped handing out answers and started asking what they'd already tried.
- Code review became about the decision, not just the diff.
- I let people own the mistake and the fix. That's where the learning actually lives.

For about two weeks we got slower. Then the team got faster than I ever was on my own.

Leadership isn't being the best engineer in the room. It's leaving behind a room full of better engineers.

Same standard as always: ship work that stays shipped — tested, monitored, and understandable to whoever maintains it next.
